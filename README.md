# buy arbitrum: ARB token or Arbitrum One, what the fees actually cost, and the mistake to avoid

Type "buy arbitrum" into a search bar and you're probably after one of two very different things. Either you want the ARB governance token as a position, or you want to actually use the Arbitrum One network — gas money, DeFi pools, an airdrop run. Those are not the same purchase, and mixing them up is the most common way people in this search lose money before they've even placed an order.

So before any exchange tabs get opened, work out which one you are.

| What you're trying to do | What you actually need | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold exposure to Arbitrum's governance token | ARB | Spot market, e.g. ARB/USDT |
| Pay gas on Arbitrum One | ETH withdrawn on the Arbitrum One network | Your own wallet on Arbitrum One |
| Farm, provide liquidity, or trade on Arbitrum dApps | ETH, USDC, or USDT withdrawn on Arbitrum One | Your own wallet on Arbitrum One |
| Bet on ARB's price with leverage | ARB perpetual futures | A derivatives account |

If you're in row two or three, buying ARB does nothing for you. ARB is not the gas token on Arbitrum One — ETH is. ARB is a governance token that sits on Ethereum and gets bridged around from there.

## What you're actually buying when you buy ARB

ARB launched with Arbitrum's token generation event in March 2023, distributed partly through an airdrop to network users. The all-time high was around $2.39 in January 2024. The all-time low came much later, near $0.07 on June 26, 2026.

Then things got interesting. ARB climbed roughly 159% over 60 days to about 20 cents by early October 2026, according to 24/7 Wall St.'s reporting on Yahoo Finance. The trigger was mostly narrative: on September 14, 2026, Standard Chartered's head of digital assets research, Geoff Kendrick, initiated coverage, called Arbitrum "hugely undervalued," set a $0.50 target for end-2026 and floated $10 by 2030.

The second part is real revenue, not vibes. Robinhood Chain runs on Arbitrum's technology and returns 10% of its net protocol income to the Arbitrum ecosystem — 8% to the DAO treasury, 2% to the developer guild. ChainCatcher, citing arbdata, put Robinhood Chain's cumulative fee income at $13.05 million in its first two months, with roughly $1.3 million of that flowing to Arbitrum.

### The part the rally posts leave out

ARB has a vesting schedule, and it's still running. Team, investor and advisor tokens were locked for a year and then released in 36 equal monthly instalments. That works out to 92,645,833 ARB per month — a figure you can rebuild from the Arbitrum Foundation's own allocation table, not just copy off an aggregator.

Depending on which model you trust, the insider lock ends between mid-February and the end of March 2027, with a separate Foundation stream running until April 17, 2027. The big cliff — around 1.11 billion ARB freed on March 16, 2024 — is already behind the market. What remains is a steady drip worth roughly 1.3% to 2.0% of circulating supply per month.

And here's the honest structural argument against ARB that shows up in most serious write-ups: it's a governance token with no claim on network fees. Holders vote on how the DAO treasury is spent. They don't receive sequencer revenue, can't stake for protocol yield, and there's no burn tied to usage. Whether that changes is a governance decision, not a product roadmap item.

None of which tells you what the price will do. It tells you what you're holding if you buy.

This is the part most "buy Arbitrum" guides skip, and it costs more than the fee difference people obsess over.

Where a smaller, thinner pair genuinely helps is the opposite scenario: when a coin only lists on two places. ARB doesn't have that problem.

## Four things the fee table doesn't tell you

**Maker and taker cost the same until VIP 4.** If your strategy is resting limit orders on the book, you get nothing back for providing liquidity at the bottom four tiers. The split only opens up at VIP 4, and stays under a single basis point all the way to VIP 9.

**Paying in GT stops helping at VIP 10.** At VIP 0 the GT rate is 0.090% against a standard 0.100% — about a tenth off. From VIP 10 upward the two columns are identical. The token discount is a beginner-to-mid-tier benefit, not a whale benefit.

**GT deduction has a silent fallback.** Gate's own fee page states that once GT deduction is switched on, GT is spent first, and if the balance runs dry the system automatically reverts to the standard VIP rate. Budget for 0.09% across a long session and you can finish it paying 0.10%.

**Withdrawal limits follow your tier, not your verification level.** The 24-hour limit climbs from $3 million at VIP 0 to $50 million at VIP 16. If you're moving large amounts off an exchange, that ceiling matters more than the trading fee.

Tier assignment itself runs on whichever is better between your 30-day weighted trading volume and your 14-day average GT holding, reassessed periodically. Volume isn't weighted equally either: spot and stocks count at 100%, USDT and BTC perpetuals at 40%, options and USD1 contracts at 20%, CFDs at 10%. Published representative thresholds put VIP 1 at $2,000 in assets, 50 GT, or $60,000 of 30-day volume, and VIP 5 at $40,000, 2,000 GT, or $1 million. Treat those as landmarks and check the live VIP page before planning anything around them.

## Getting your ARB out without wasting money

Withdrawal fees on Gate aren't a flat percentage. They're set per coin and per network, and re-priced roughly hourly against network congestion. That means the same ARB balance can cost wildly different amounts to move depending on which chain you pick. Ethereum mainnet is the expensive end; L2 networks and cheap L1s sit at the bottom.

Three habits that actually reduce the bill:

- **Consolidate.** Two withdrawals cost more than one withdrawal of double the size, every time.
- **Check the network status field before sending.** The deposit-withdrawal table lists networks that are temporarily deposit-disabled. A failed transfer is a support ticket, not a rounding error.
- **Use GateCode for internal moves.** Sending to another Gate user costs nothing, which makes it a reasonable way to hand ARB to someone without touching the chain at all.

## FAQ

**Is ARB the same as Arbitrum ETH?**
No. ARB is the governance token, launched in March 2023. ETH on Arbitrum One is the asset you use for gas and most DeFi activity on the network. Different assets, different jobs.

**Can I buy ARB with a credit card?**
Gate offers a card channel for ARB where bank transfer isn't available in your currency, and states that fees apply on each transaction. P2P and on-chain deposits are usually cheaper, at the cost of a few extra minutes.

**Do I need to complete KYC?**
Yes, for the payment and full trading features. Gate's own ARB guide lists verification as a required step before choosing a purchase method.

**Is there a minimum purchase?**
It depends on the channel. P2P, card and spot orders each have their own minimums and they display before you confirm. Read the number on the preview screen rather than assuming.

**What actually moves ARB's price?**
Unlock schedules, ecosystem revenue routed back to the DAO, and analyst coverage all show up in the order flow. Governance decisions on whether holders ever receive a direct revenue share are the structural question, and it hasn't been resolved.

**Is ARB available in the US?**
Region rules vary by platform and campaign. Some Gate promotions explicitly exclude US, UK and mainland China users. ARB is listed on US-regulated venues, but the comps there are a different article.
